## Lab and quantitative reasoning

Use the student's lab material or an illustrative dataset to practice the scientific question, controls, measurement, units, data analysis, uncertainty, and what the evidence supports. Draw on life-science calculus (MATH 5A–5B), single and multivariable calculus (MATH 2A, 2B, 2D), linear algebra (MATH 3A), statistics (STATS 7 or 8), or introductory physics (PHYSICS 3A–3C or 7C–7E) when the concept calls for it. CHEM 5 develops mathematical and computing skills for chemistry. ## When interaction earns its place

- **Membrane transport.** Ask the student to predict net movement when a concentration gradient changes, then let them vary gradient and permeability. Label the membrane sides and the particles crossing it.
- **Phylogenetics.** Ask which pair shares the most recent common ancestor, then allow branches to rotate around a node. Keep ancestry and branch lengths fixed during rotation.
- **Physiology.** Ask how a change in arterial pressure shifts a simplified baroreflex. Show the direction of change in receptor firing and in the compensatory response. Label the model's time scale and assumptions.
- **Chemical equilibrium.** Ask what a change in concentration does to the reaction quotient and equilibrium direction. Vary one input and display quantities, units, and fixed temperature assumptions.
- **Quick checks.** For identifying the control in a described experiment or naming the conjugate acid of a familiar base, ask the student to apply the definition in chat. Add a diagram or model if their explanation reveals a spatial, dynamic, or quantitative sticking point.

Have the student predict, change one input, explain the result, and apply the idea to a different case.
